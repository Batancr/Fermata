Privacy

Last updated 15 October 2026

In short: Fermata has no ads and no tracking of its own, and sets no cookies. What you make in Fermata is kept in your browser. If you sign in, a copy is kept in your account so your devices match. Videos play through YouTube, which has its own privacy rules. You can delete everything yourself in Settings → Your data.

What's kept in your browser

Fermata saves what you make on this device, in your browser's own storage (local storage, and IndexedDB for music files). It stays there until you delete it, and it isn't sent anywhere unless you sign in.

  • Your music list (YouTube video links, titles and lengths) and your playlists
  • Your timers, projects, the time you've tracked, your daily log and sleep log
  • Your settings, Fermats and what you've picked up in the shop
  • Music files you add from your device, and a short log of when the music stopped for an ad (used to time around ads)
  • Your own YouTube API key, if you add one. It never leaves this device.
  • Your rough location, only if you tap Use my location in Day mode (for sunrise and sunset times). It's rounded to about 11 km before it's saved, and Forget my location removes it. Without it, Fermata guesses from your time zone.

What's kept in your account (only if you sign in)

Accounts are optional. They use Supabase, a database service, and are shared with Vamos, a game by the same maker, so one sign-in works on both.

  • Your sign-in: your email address and username. Your password goes to Supabase, which handles signing in; Fermata doesn't keep it. Your sign-in is remembered in this browser's storage until you sign out.
  • Your Fermata copy: one private record with everything in the list above, except music files (they're too big; only their names sync) and your own YouTube API key.
  • Only you can read or change your record once signed in. Nobody can see it while signed out.

YouTube

Videos play in YouTube's own player inside Fermata, using YouTube's privacy-enhanced mode (youtube-nocookie.com). YouTube describes this mode as not storing information about visitors until they play a video. Once a video plays, YouTube (part of Google) can see that it played and may use cookies or similar storage, as on any site with a YouTube video. Any ads in videos are YouTube's, not Fermata's.

To find titles and lengths, Fermata asks YouTube's data service about the videos and playlists you add. Google's privacy policy covers what YouTube and Google do with this.

Other services involved

  • GitHub Pages hosts the site. Like most web hosts, it may keep basic logs such as IP addresses. See GitHub's privacy statement.
  • jsDelivr, a public file service, delivers the code that talks to Supabase.

Fermata doesn't sell or share your data with anyone else, and doesn't use analytics.

How to delete it

  • Settings → Your data → Delete my data… lets you delete Fermata's copy in your account, everything Fermata keeps in this browser, or both. It can't be undone.
  • If Fermata is signed in on another device, sign out there first, or it puts its copy back the next time it syncs.
  • Your sign-in itself is shared with Vamos, so deleting Fermata's data leaves it in place.
  • Clearing this site's data in your browser's settings also removes everything kept in this browser.

Changes and questions

If this page changes, the date at the top changes too. For questions about your data, open an issue on Fermata's GitHub page. Issues there are public, so please don't include personal details; ask for a private way to reply if you need one.
